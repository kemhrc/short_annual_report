Neonatal Rotavirus Vaccination Study

An open-label, randomized, controlled trial testing whether a birth-dose schedule of oral rotavirus vaccine protects as well as the standard WHO infant schedule — across all four WHO-prequalified vaccines, in 1,200 neonates from the CHRU catchment area, Pune.

Study design — 8 randomized arms
Primary outcome

Proportion of infants seroresponding (IgA ≥20 U/mL) 4 weeks after final dose.

Secondary outcomes

Antibody levels (GMC), viral shedding, maternal-antibody influence, safety (AEs/SAEs).

FY2025–26 VISITORS INCLUDED TEAMS FROM ICMR, RMRC DIBRUGARH, D.Y. PATIL UNIVERSITY, MAHAN TRUST MELGHAT, RESPIRE (UPM / UM / UK), STATE HEALTH SYSTEM RESOURCE CENTRE, AND UNIVERSITY OF SRI JAYEWARDENEPURA, SRI LANKA.

Section 09

Key Projects

Collaborative research, vaccine evaluation, and community engagement projects advancing maternal and child health across research and implementation settings.

03
Collaborative research platform
PRERNA 3.0 — Platform for Research Excellence Related to National Aims

PRERNA 3.0 aims to establish a collaborative research platform to nurture early-career researchers and accelerate high-quality, interdisciplinary public health research in maternal and child health.

Objectives

Build researcher capacity through mentorship and structured training; identify priority research questions via evidence synthesis and secondary data analysis; and develop multicentric research proposals for India's maternal and child health priorities.

Expected outcomes · strengthened institutional research capacity
Phase IV vaccine trial
Neonatal Rotavirus Vaccination Study

KEMHRC, Pune, is conducting a Phase IV trial to evaluate whether a birth dose of oral rotavirus vaccine is as effective and safe as the standard WHO schedule.

The study compares immune responses across 8 groups (4 vaccines × 2 schedules) in 1,200 neonates. Findings will help shape India's rotavirus vaccination policy.

Community engagement
Infosys Foundation's Community Engagement Model for Maternal and Child Health

The KEMHRC project targets maternal and child health in 64 tribal villages of Maharashtra, using Arogya Kutis, SNEH centers, mobile clinics, and trained community health workers to overcome access barriers.

It aims to raise MCH awareness by 70% and improve quality of life by 50%, while building local capacity and income generation for sustainability through culturally sensitive, community-driven primary healthcare.

Section 10

Outreach

Community health, clinical support, scientific exchange, and institutional review beyond the research portfolio.

04
Impact
Cleft lip and palate care

Children from Nandurbar, Dhule, and Pune districts with cleft lip and palate received free counseling and surgical treatment at KEM Hospital through KEMHRC. The program focuses on early detection and support so every child can receive the care they need at no cost.

Diabetes
Supporting children with Type 1 diabetes

Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune condition that destroys insulin-producing cells and requires lifelong daily insulin injections, particularly in children and adolescents. With insulin therapy, monitoring, and lifestyle management, patients can live healthy lives, but limited access to insulin and healthcare can lead to severe complications and early death.
